What this shows:

- The model does sort trades: the actual win rate rises from 31.8% for all 2,673 validation trades to 41.5% for its top 20%, and its predicted win rates are close to the actual ones.
- But no cut is profitable after costs. The best is the top 25% at -0.077R per trade (669 trades). Spread is already inside the gross R (-0.160R for all trades); slippage takes another 0.051R per trade on average.
- Every slice wins less often than it needs to. 'Needed win' is the break-even win rate from that slice's own average winner and loser (a winner averages well under 2R because many trades end at the 6-hour time limit with a small gain or loss). At the top 25% the model wins 40.8% but needs 44.9%; the gap is never closed.
- The root cause is the label. The model is trained on 'win' = the trade ended above 0R, so it learns to pick setups that drift slightly up and close at the time limit, not setups that reach the 2R target. The stricter the cut, the more of its wins are small time-limit wins (35% of all wins, 78% in the top 15%) and the less often the 2R target is hit (20.4% of all trades, 9.0% in the top 15%). A higher win rate made of smaller wins does not pay for the losers.
